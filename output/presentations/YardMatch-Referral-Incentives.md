# YardMatch referral incentives

Prepared September 27, 2026. Proposed pilot terms for owner review. This adds an execution plan; it does not activate rewards, change app pricing, or change the investor forecast.

## Recommended offers

Use two incentives to grow customer demand and verified provider capacity together. Start in Barnegat and expand into adjacent covered neighborhoods after the existing reliability and contribution gates pass.

| Program | Proposed reward | When it is earned |
|---|---|---|
| Customer introduces a neighbor | $20 reward to the existing customer and $10 to the new household | The new household completes its first eligible service and payment to the provider is verified, followed by a 14-day validation period |
| Provider introduces another business | $50 credit against the referring provider's future YardMatch finder-fee invoices | The new business passes normal verification, completes paid work for three distinct new YardMatch households, and pays its first two non-waived finder fees |

Customer rewards are YardMatch-funded digital gift cards, subject to selection of a fulfillment vendor. They are separate from the provider's service invoice. The provider receives the full agreed service payment directly from the homeowner. The provider referral credit has no cash value and cannot exceed the balance of future finder-fee invoices. The new provider keeps the existing one-client finder-fee waiver; that waiver is already part of the acquisition model.

These amounts are proposed test inputs, not established market rates. The expansion documents assume $75 / $110 / $150 finder fees and direct homeowner-to-provider payments. The application's existing settlement and fee behavior differs from that planning model. Implementation must reconcile the selected billing model before promising or issuing these incentives.

## Why this can accelerate expansion

- Existing customers invite nearby households after a completed service. Nearby jobs can use the same verified provider capacity and help fill routes.
- Established providers introduce other local firms, reducing prospecting effort while preserving ordinary verification and service standards.
- Referred households enter the normal service, timing, property, and recommendation flow. Referral rewards never determine provider ranking or create an open provider directory.
- Demand referrals open only in covered service areas with spare capacity. Referrals into future areas join an opt-in waitlist and receive no reward promise until the area opens and an eligible offer is accepted.

Lawn Love's current support documentation places referral sharing inside its customer app and uses a promotional first-mow offer. This supports testing an in-product referral path, but does not establish YardMatch's reward size, conversion, CAC, or retention. [Lawn Love referral documentation](https://support.lawnlove.com/hc/en-us/articles/24416620838418--Customer-How-to-Receive-Promotional-Offers).

## Pilot budget and economics

Run an eight-week enrollment pilot, capped at 100 qualifying new households and five qualifying provider referrals. Reserve funding when an eligible referral is accepted. Honor accepted offers after enrollment closes, including the completion and validation windows.

| Item | Calculation | Maximum pilot allowance |
|---|---|---:|
| Customer rewards | 100 × ($20 + $10) | $3,000 |
| Reward delivery, verification and exception allowance | 100 × $5 | $500 |
| Referral setup and creative | Fixed | $500 |
| Provider finder-fee credits | 5 × $50 | $250 |
| Total incremental economic allowance | Cash allowance plus foregone finder revenue | **$4,250** |

The cash allowance is $4,000. The $250 provider-credit allowance reduces future finder-fee collections; it is not an additional cash payout. Reserve the full face value of promised credits without assuming breakage. Existing welcome waivers, ordinary provider onboarding, payroll, and service support remain separate costs in the operating model.

At 100 qualifying household conversions, customer referral CAC is $40: $35 variable cost plus $5 allocated setup cost. At 50 conversions it is $45, and at 25 it is $55, assuming the $500 setup allowance is spent. Measure actual total program spending, including rejected-claim handling and unrecovered rewards, rather than assuming the $5 allowance will always suffice.

A typical $75 collected finder fee leaves $35 after $40 referral CAC, before welcome-waiver allocation, provider acquisition, service support, billing costs, and other variable costs. That is not contribution profit. The existing $20 contribution gate leaves at most $15 for those other costs on this simple typical-property example. Evaluate waived relationships as zero finder revenue and include them in the cohort result.

The current forecast's $55 initial customer CAC already includes incentives. Do not add all referral costs on top of that figure or assume $55 is a measured paid-channel CAC. Replace the blended placeholder with explicit channel spending only after the channel baseline is known.

For illustration only, if non-referral CAC were $55 and referral CAC were $40, a 30% referral mix would produce $50.50 blended CAC. The same acquisition budget could acquire approximately 8.9% more households ($55 / $50.50 − 1), subject to available demand and provider capacity. These are scenario calculations, not forecast growth or proof of incrementality.

At a future $30 non-referral CAC, this $40 referral program would be more expensive. Re-test reward sizes and conversion before extending the offer nationally.

## Eligibility and reward handling

- An existing customer with a verified completed service can refer a first-time YardMatch household. A qualifying household has no prior completed YardMatch service; a second account or property owned by the same household does not create another acquisition reward.
- One customer referral offer per new household. Attach the referral before the first booking; preserve original marketing attribution separately. No stacking with another new-household acquisition incentive.
- The new household accepts the offer and books within 30 days, then completes its first service within 60 days of acceptance. Disclose those dates before acceptance. Ops can document an extension for YardMatch/provider-caused delays.
- Reward eligibility requires completed work, customer confirmation of direct payment, and corroborating provider/invoice evidence. A booking marked complete alone is insufficient. Avoid collecting full bank statements or unrelated payment information.
- After the 14-day validation period, issue earned gift cards within seven days. An unresolved payment or service dispute pauses eligibility with a visible reason and an ops owner. Cancellation, fabricated work, or refunded service before qualification does not earn rewards. A later issue is reviewed by ops; do not silently debit customers or providers.
- Cap the pilot at five rewarded household referrals per referrer and three provider credits per referring business. Related businesses under common ownership, self-referrals, employee-controlled accounts, and manufactured household identities are ineligible. Identity/address matches trigger review, rather than automatically rejecting unrelated people sharing a device or address.
- The provider activation window is 90 days from offer acceptance. Record the three qualifying households and two collected, non-waived finder invoices. Each new business can have only one referring business.
- Stop accepting new offers when budget reservations reach the cap. A budget pause does not cancel accepted, eligible offers. Capacity and economics gates govern new offers and expansion, not payment of rewards already earned.
- Invitation copy states that the sender can earn a reward. Customers share their own links; do not upload address books or send unsolicited messages to a referred person. No reward requires a positive review, public endorsement, or social post.

## Eight-week operating test

1. **Prepare:** approve reward terms and budget, select fulfillment, establish eligibility records and an auditable reward register, and check provider capacity. Configure unique referral codes and campaign/area identifiers.
2. **Weeks 1–2:** invite a small cohort of customers with completed services and verified provider owners. Release at most 25–50 household offers at a time. Check identity, work/payment evidence, support effort, and reward delivery manually.
3. **Weeks 3–4:** continue only where matches and completion remain reliable. Compare randomly assigned eligible existing customers receiving the offer with a contemporaneous group receiving the ordinary referral invitation without an incentive. Keep acquisition eligibility, observation windows, and service coverage comparable.
4. **Weeks 5–8:** review referred bookings and matured completions, committed rewards, actual spending, active supply, and early rebooking. Allow outstanding accepted offers to mature before calculating final conversion economics.
5. **After maturity:** decide whether to increase the cap, change the reward, or stop new enrollment. Wait for the 90-day provider and second-season retention evidence before claiming those retention gates have passed.

The first 100 conversions are an operational and economic pilot. They may not establish a statistically reliable growth effect. Track spillovers between neighborhoods and report cohort sizes and uncertainty. Do not count a shifted organic booking as incremental acquisition merely because it used a code.

## Weekly scorecard and expansion decision

Track invitations delivered, unique eligible referral claims, first bookings, verified paid completions, pending/earned/issued rewards, reward delivery time, fraud and exception costs, customer CAC, provider activation cost, days to first service, repeat use, and incremental completed relationships per invited customer versus the holdout. Separate earned rewards from unearned reservations.

Expand the program only when:

- actual referral CAC is no higher than the comparable non-referral channel and within the local CAC ceiling;
- contribution remains at least $20 per acquired relationship after waivers, customer acquisition, allocated provider acquisition including referral credits, billing, and first-season support;
- completion remains at least 92%, provider-caused cancellations/rework/material issues remain at or below 5%, and capacity and curated-backup gates continue to pass;
- the holdout supports incremental completed relationships rather than only attribution changes;
- reward delivery and reconciliation work reliably, and the budget is funded for accepted offers.

Use the existing national expansion gates in addition to these tests. Increase acquisition batches where capacity exists; change the service-area launch schedule only after the area itself qualifies.

## Financial-model integration

The next workbook revision should replace the current blended customer CAC placeholder with a channel build:

`Customer acquisition spend = non-referral acquisition spend + customer rewards earned + referral delivery/verification spend + allocated referral setup spend`

`Blended customer CAC = total customer acquisition spend / all first-time completed paying households`

Count each household once across channels. Allocate setup once and reconcile it to the launch/marketing budget. Keep growth payroll and ordinary support in their existing lines. Compare holdout-based incremental CAC separately from attributed CAC.

Show provider reward credits as a separate deduction from finder-fee revenue, with an earned-credit roll-forward, application to invoices, and cash-collection timing. Include that economic cost in provider acquisition reporting without also subtracting it as a second P&L expense. Keep the existing welcome waiver separate. Final accounting presentation needs the company's accounting policy.

Do not increase both new-household volume and reduce CAC for the same benefit unless an explicit budget/capacity model supports both. Preserve the existing footprint and household forecast until pilot evidence justifies a change. The current workbook and deck do not yet include this referral schedule.

## Application work after terms are selected

September 28 implementation checkpoint: customer web and mobile forms now save email/US-phone referrals with authenticated ownership, contact permission, normalized-contact deduplication, a rolling daily limit, an atomic linked ops lead, and coarse customer-visible progress. Customers open a prepared invitation in their own email/text app. Booking attribution reuses the lead/contact matching workflow. No message delivery or reward fulfillment is claimed. Details and verification commands are in `README.md` and `LOCAL_DEVELOPMENT.md`.

A complete reward program still needs:

- persistent referral codes, immutable offer versions, acceptance dates, area eligibility, attribution, and budget reservations;
- reward eligibility/status/terms in the customer views and a provider referral flow;
- role-aware ops review with evidence, reasons, actor/time history, and a separate reward ledger;
- one-time qualification and issuance constraints, safe retries, reconciliation, holds, and explicit reversals;
- a gift-card fulfillment integration and a finder-invoice credit path consistent with the selected billing model;
- regression coverage for duplicate claims, concurrent qualification, self-referrals, waived finder fees, disputed/refunded work, budget caps, provider isolation, and reward delivery failures.

Owner decisions before publishing the offer: reward amounts and gift-card format, pilot budget/caps, eligibility windows, and the billing model that will support provider credits. No customer-facing offer or reward obligation has been activated by this document.
