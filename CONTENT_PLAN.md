# YardMatch Content Plan

Last updated: June 17, 2026

This is the working content plan for YardMatch's launch-stage SEO and trust-building work.

The purpose of this plan is to decide:

- what content YardMatch should publish first
- which content is for homeowners versus providers
- which content should support bookings directly
- which content should wait until the business has stronger traction

Related docs:

- `SEO_PLAN.md`
- `MARKETING_PLAN.md`
- `COSTS.md`

## Core Recommendation

YardMatch should not start with a giant generic blog.

The better launch approach is:

1. build high-intent local landing pages first
2. add a small content hub only after the booking pages are in place
3. prioritize homeowner demand content over broad provider thought leadership
4. publish provider-growth content only if provider acquisition becomes an immediate need

Recommended content section name:

- `/resources/`

This is better than a big broad `/blog/` at the start because it keeps the focus on useful, practical content rather than volume for its own sake.

## Content Goals

The first content wave should help YardMatch do four things:

- rank for local service-intent searches
- answer questions that block booking
- build trust after someone sees the brand elsewhere
- support future expansion into nearby towns

## Content Tracks

Use two separate content tracks.

### Track 1: Homeowner Content

This is the primary track for launch.

Use it to attract:

- homeowners
- landlords
- seasonal residents
- families looking for convenient lawn care help

This content should tie directly back to booking intent.

### Track 2: Provider Content

This is the secondary track.

Use it only if:

- provider acquisition becomes a bottleneck
- you want organic provider signups
- you want to build the provider-side brand over time

This content should tie back to provider signup and growth pages, not customer booking pages.

## Publishing Priority

Priority order:

1. local town pages
2. service pages
3. service + town pages
4. homeowner FAQ and guide content
5. provider acquisition content

## Track 1: Homeowner Content Plan

This is the most important content for the first 60 to 90 days.

### Tier 1: Must-Have Booking Pages

These are not optional if SEO is supposed to drive local demand.

Recommended first pages:

1. `Barnegat, NJ Lawn Care and Landscaping`
2. `Lawn Mowing in Barnegat, NJ`
3. `Yard Cleanup in Barnegat, NJ`
4. `Hedge Trimming in Barnegat, NJ`
5. `Mulch Refresh in Barnegat, NJ`

Purpose:

- capture high-intent search traffic
- give direct mail and social traffic a strong landing page
- reinforce the Barnegat launch story

### Tier 2: Homeowner FAQ Content

These pieces should answer the practical questions that stop someone from booking.

Recommended topics:

- How often should you schedule lawn mowing in Barnegat, NJ?
- What is included in a yard cleanup service?
- When should homeowners schedule hedge trimming in coastal New Jersey?
- What affects the price of lawn care in Barnegat?
- Should you book weekly or biweekly lawn service?
- What should you ask before hiring a local landscaper?

Best format:

- short, useful guide pages
- FAQ sections attached to service pages
- not separate articles unless the topic deserves its own page

### Tier 3: Seasonal Homeowner Guides

These are useful after the core service pages are live.

Recommended topics:

- Spring yard cleanup checklist for Barnegat homeowners
- When to book mulch refresh in Barnegat
- Fall yard cleanup timing for Ocean County homes
- How to prepare your yard before a first mowing visit
- Signs your lawn care schedule is too infrequent

These should link directly to booking pages.

## Track 2: Provider Content Plan

This track matters, but it should not outrank the homeowner content queue during launch.

### Best Provider Topics

If provider acquisition becomes important, start here:

1. How to get more landscaping clients in a small local market
2. How to build route density for a lawn mowing business
3. How to price recurring lawn mowing work
4. When to hire seasonal lawn care crew
5. How landscaping businesses can reduce scheduling chaos
6. What small landscaping companies should track before buying leads

### Topics To Delay

These may eventually matter, but they are not first-wave content:

- broad entrepreneurship content
- generic startup motivation articles
- national landscaping industry trend summaries
- content written mainly because competitors have a blog

### "How To Start A Landscaping Business"

This topic can help provider-side SEO, but it is not a launch priority for YardMatch.

Why it should wait:

- it is top-of-funnel and broad
- it does not directly help Barnegat homeowner bookings
- it attracts a wider audience than the current local launch needs
- it can distract from more commercially useful local content

When it becomes worth doing:

- when YardMatch is intentionally growing the provider side
- when the provider pages are stronger
- when there is enough bandwidth to produce real, high-quality provider education

## Recommended Content Structure

Keep the content architecture clean and separated by audience.

Recommended structure:

- `/barnegat-nj`
- `/barnegat-nj/lawn-mowing`
- `/barnegat-nj/yard-cleanup`
- `/services/lawn-mowing`
- `/services/yard-cleanup`
- `/resources/homeowners/...`
- `/resources/providers/...`

This makes it easier to keep customer intent and provider intent separate.

## Content Templates

To keep quality consistent, use simple templates.

### Local Service Page Template

Recommended sections:

- H1 with service + town
- short intro
- what the service includes
- common reasons people book it
- when to book it
- why YardMatch
- FAQ
- call to action

### Homeowner Guide Template

Recommended sections:

- clear H1
- short direct answer
- practical details
- common mistakes or questions
- local context where relevant
- next step or booking call to action

### Provider Guide Template

Recommended sections:

- clear H1
- the operational problem
- practical advice
- common mistakes
- how YardMatch fits in
- provider signup or learn-more CTA

## Tone And Quality Rules

Every piece should feel like it was written for a real person with a real goal.

Use:

- practical language
- real local context
- specific examples
- clear next steps

Avoid:

- filler intros
- repetitive keyword stuffing
- obvious AI-sounding phrasing
- rewriting the same article for many towns
- publishing just to hit a volume target

## Internal Linking Rules

Every content page should support a business outcome.

Homeowner pages should link to:

- the Barnegat page
- relevant service pages
- booking or marketplace pages

Provider pages should link to:

- provider landing pages
- provider signup pages
- relevant provider feature pages

Do not send provider readers to customer booking pages unless there is a very specific reason.

## What Not To Publish Yet

Hold off on these for now:

- broad national blog content
- dozens of city pages with near-duplicate copy
- trend-chasing articles unrelated to actual demand
- generic "top 10 tips" content with no local or operational angle
- a huge archive of low-intent provider career content

## Success Criteria

This content plan is working if:

- the Barnegat pages start earning impressions
- service pages support bookings
- branded search trust improves
- homeowners land on useful pages and convert
- content production stays focused instead of sprawling

If we want the highest leverage first, do these next:
